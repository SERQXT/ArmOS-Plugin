# SOW Reviewer — Antagonistic Statement of Work Review

An intentionally skeptical, thorough review of a Statement of Work. This skill approaches every SOW as a future delivery team would — looking for gaps, ambiguities, inconsistencies, scope surprises, commercial misalignments, and commitments that cannot be kept. It surfaces problems before the customer signs, not after.

## Review Philosophy — Assume Nothing, Question Everything

This skill is designed to be the last line of defense before a SOW reaches a customer. It operates from a delivery perspective: if something in this SOW would cause a problem during delivery, it should be caught here.

**The five review stances:**

1. **Completeness Adversary.** Every required section must be present, populated, and specific. Vague language, TBDs, and missing fields are cited regardless of context.
2. **Scope Accuracy Auditor.** What the SOW says will be delivered must precisely match what was agreed in the Discovery Record and Scope Model. Over-promises and under-defines are equally dangerous.
3. **Commercial Accuracy Validator.** Hours in the SOW must match the LOE Estimate. Fees must match the Commercial Recommendation. Inconsistencies create contractual and revenue risk.
4. **Risk Spotter.** Anything that could cause delivery failure, budget overrun, scope creep, or customer disappointment is flagged — even if it is technically present in the SOW.
5. **Format Compliance Inspector (Domo SOWs only).** For Domo-generated SOWs, verify that the DOMO SOW TEMPLATE 2025 structure, section order, fonts, colors, and images are preserved as required.

---

## Two Review Modes

### Mode A: Domo-Format SOW Review
Use when reviewing a SOW generated by Domo's sow-generator skill or following the DOMO SOW TEMPLATE 2025.

**Additional checks apply:**
- Template fidelity (section order matches template exactly)
- Standard boilerplate sections unaltered (Delivery Methodology, Change Request Process, Other Terms)
- Font, color, image, and formatting preservation
- Placeholder resolution (no unresolved `[PLACEHOLDER]` fields in the final version)

### Mode B: Partner / Third-Party SOW Review
Use when reviewing a SOW provided by a partner, customer, or third-party system integrator that does not follow the Domo SOW format.

**Adjusted approach:**
- Do not apply Domo template structure checks — the format will differ
- Map the partner SOW's sections to the equivalent Domo SOW sections to assess coverage
- Evaluate scope coverage, commercial alignment, and delivery feasibility regardless of format
- Flag sections that are standard in Domo SOWs but missing or unclear in the partner document
- Identify any commitments in the partner SOW that Domo would be expected to fulfill that are not reflected in the Domo Scope Model

### Step 3: Scope Accuracy Review

Compare what the SOW says will be delivered against the locked Scope Model, Discovery Record, and Solution Blueprint. Look for four types of misalignment:

#### 3a. Over-Promising — SOW promises more than the Scope Model

The SOW commits to deliverables that are not in the locked Scope Model. These become binding contractual commitments for delivery work that was not estimated or resourced.

*How to find:* Read every deliverable list in the SOW. Cross-reference against the Scope Model's in-scope list. Any item in the SOW but not in the Scope Model is an over-promise.

*Severity:*
- 🔴 **CRITICAL** — Major deliverables present in SOW but absent from Scope Model (hours, use cases, platforms)
- 🟡 **IMPORTANT** — Minor deliverables or language that implies more than the scope intends
- 🟢 **ADVISORY** — Wording that is ambiguous but unlikely to cause scope creep

#### 3b. Under-Defining — SOW omits key details that the Scope Model contains

The SOW lacks specificity that the Scope Model defines. This creates ambiguity that the customer will interpret in their favor during delivery.

*How to find:* Read the Scope Model deliverables. Look for each in the SOW. If the SOW covers the workstream but omits the deliverable count, names, or specifications that the Scope Model defines, that is under-defining.

*Examples:*
- Scope Model: "15 dashboards across 3 business units." SOW: "Dashboards will be built." → Under-defined.
- Scope Model: "3 Magic ETL DataFlows for sales, finance, ops." SOW: "ETL pipelines will be developed." → Under-defined.

*Severity:*
- 🔴 **CRITICAL** — Missing quantities, names, or deliverable specifications for major workstreams
- 🟡 **IMPORTANT** — Missing specifications that could lead to scope disagreements
- 🟢 **ADVISORY** — Minor gaps in specificity unlikely to cause disputes

#### 3c. Scope Creep Setup — Language that opens doors for expanded scope

The SOW contains language (explicit or implicit) that the customer could reasonably use to justify additional work at no charge.

*How to find:* Look for:
- "As needed" / "As required" / "Reasonable requests" language without quantification
- "And similar" / "And related" / "Including but not limited to" without bounding language
- "Best efforts" without specific deliverables
- Missing out-of-scope language (if the SOW doesn't say something is out of scope, the customer assumes it is in)
- Open-ended revision language without defined rounds or caps

*Severity:*
- 🔴 **CRITICAL** — Language that is unambiguously a blank check for additional scope
- 🟡 **IMPORTANT** — Language that a customer could reasonably interpret as including more
- 🟢 **ADVISORY** — Language that is mildly ambiguous but unlikely to be exploited

#### 3d. Out-of-Scope Alignment — What the SOW excludes vs. what the customer expects

Check the Engagement Assumptions section for standard exclusions. Cross-reference against Discovery Record open questions and items parked as out-of-scope.

*Key questions:*
- Are all items from the Scope Model's out-of-scope list reflected in the SOW (as exclusions or assumptions)?
- Does the SOW explicitly exclude scope that was declined during commercial discussions?
- Are the standard Domo PS exclusions present (heavy data cleansing, BI tool migration, SSO, custom connectors unless specifically scoped, etc.)?

### Step 5: Risk and Delivery Feasibility Review

Review the SOW through the lens of a delivery team inheriting this engagement. What will cause problems?

**Risk assessment areas:**

#### Customer Responsibility Gaps
- Are customer responsibilities named (person, not role), dated (specific date, not "in time"), and consequenced ("if not received by [date], timeline will shift")?
- Are all critical customer dependencies called out (data access, IT involvement, decision authority)?
- Is the customer's bandwidth commitment realistic given what is expected of them?

#### Assumption Quality
- Are assumptions specific and verifiable, or are they vague hedges?
- Do the assumptions adequately protect Domo from scope expansion if they prove false?
- Are assumptions numbered and traceable to specific workstreams?
- Are there missing assumptions the Scope Model identified that are not in the SOW?

#### Commitment Traps
- Does the SOW contain any commitments made verbally (from Gong transcript) that are inconsistent with the SOW language?
- Are there timeline commitments ("ready by board meeting," "live before quarter end") that the SOW delivery schedule may not support?
- Does the SOW create any implied warranties about data quality, third-party systems, or business outcomes?

#### Technical Risk Flags
- Are there workstreams scoped with LOW LOE confidence that are in a fixed-bid SOW without protecting assumptions?
- Are there third-party dependencies (customer's IT team, external APIs, data quality) that are not adequately assumed or excluded?
- Are there automation or App Studio workstreams whose complexity is material and whose assumptions are insufficient?

#### Staffing and Timing Risks
- Does the scope require specialist skills (Workbench, DDX, Code Engine, AI) that must be flagged for staffing?
- Is the timeline compressed to the point where resource availability becomes a risk?

### Step 6: Template Fidelity Review (Mode A — Domo SOWs only)

For SOWs generated using the DOMO SOW TEMPLATE 2025:

**Structure check:**
- Are all sections present in the correct order per the template?
- Have any sections been merged, split, or reordered?
- Are any standard boilerplate sections (Delivery Methodology, Change Request Process, Other Terms) altered, summarized, or paraphrased?

**Formatting check:**
- Are section headers formatted with the correct Domo corporate color (#1A5CA8 primary, #00A0F0 accent)?
- Is body text in the correct font (Calibri, template-specified size)?
- Are tables formatted to match the template (not reformatted or restructured)?

**Branding check:**
- Is the Domo logo present on the cover page and in the header/footer?
- Are all section separator graphics and branded elements in their original positions?
- Has any image been removed, replaced, or resized?

**Placeholder resolution check:**
- Are there any unresolved placeholder fields remaining in the document?
- Are any fields populated with "[TBD]" or vague placeholder text that must be specific?

## Review Stance — Why Antagonistic?

The word "antagonistic" is intentional. This review is not designed to find reasons the SOW is acceptable — it is designed to find every way it could fail. Delivery teams inherit what is written. Customers use SOW language to defend requests. The harder the review, the safer the delivery.

**The reviewer asks:**
- "If I were a hostile customer's lawyer, what would I use from this SOW to justify additional work?"
- "If I were the delivery PM starting next Monday, what would I not know from reading this SOW?"
- "If the LOE estimate was off by 20% on one workstream, what in this SOW would protect Domo?"
- "If the customer's project lead leaves next month, would the new person have enough context from this SOW to hold a kickoff?"

If any of these questions reveals a problem, it goes into the report.

---

## Guardrails

- **Both modes must be supported.** The review works on Domo-format and partner-format SOWs. Do not decline to review a non-Domo-format SOW.
- **Citation is required.** Every issue cited must include the exact SOW language being flagged and its location. Vague critique without citation is not useful.
- **Severity must be assigned.** Every issue is CRITICAL, IMPORTANT, or ADVISORY. No issue is listed without a severity rating.
- **Resolution must be specific.** Every issue has a specific recommended action — not "improve this section" but "replace paragraph 2 of Section 3b with [specific language]."
- **The Completeness Score is calculated, not estimated.** Apply the weighted scoring rubric; do not assign a score by intuition.
- **The verdict follows the findings.** If there are CRITICAL issues, the verdict is REVISE BEFORE SEND — not CONDITIONALLY APPROVED. The verdict is determined by the finding severity, not by how close the SOW is to being right.
- **This review is not the SOW Generator.** The reviewer identifies and recommends; it does not rewrite the SOW. Revisions are performed by the SSD using the recommendations.
